About the numbers

Dissimilarity measures how much a district’s precincts differ from the district as a whole (0 = every precinct matches). It is population-weighted, assuming non-voters vote like the voters in their own precinct.

Optimized districts come from a computer search (GerryChain/ReCom) that keeps districts contiguous and within ±0.5% of equal population while minimizing dissimilarity. They are an exploration, not proposed or legally valid maps: they ignore the Voting Rights Act, county lines and communities of interest. Optimized district numbers are arbitrary.

Seats vs. votes: the seat percentages count districts won by Biden in 2020 and are compared with Biden’s share of the two-party presidential vote in the same precincts (the points shown are seat share minus vote share). Zoomed out, each state is shaded by the average dissimilarity of its districts, weighted by population.

Precinct data is from 2020, so it only roughly matches the current district lines. Enacted plans are rebuilt from whole precincts (about 1% off equal population, with a few split precincts reassigned). Winners are by 2020 presidential votes, not House results.
